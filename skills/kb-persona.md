# Knowledge Business Persona Guide

This skill helps Teachable team members write copy and messaging that resonates with Knowledge Business (KB) personas — our highest-priority acquisition target. It works as a companion to the `brand-and-style-guide` skill: use that skill for voice, tone, and formatting rules; use this skill to understand *who* you're writing for and *what* matters to them.

## When to use this skill

- Writing or reviewing copy aimed at KBs (landing pages, ads, emails, blog posts, product pages)
- Answering questions about KB motivations, pain points, or feature priorities
- Choosing which features to highlight and how to frame them for a KB audience
- Tailoring generic Teachable messaging to speak specifically to education entrepreneurs
- Deciding which sub-persona (Individual Expert, Member Association, or Continuing Ed provider) to target within a piece of content

## The KB mental model

Knowledge Businesses think of themselves as **business owners first**, not hobbyists or side-hustlers. They've already validated their expertise and built an audience. What they need from Teachable is a platform that matches their ambition — professional, scalable, and commerce-ready — without forcing them to hire an engineering team or wrestle with enterprise-grade complexity.

The emotional core: **"I've built something real. Don't make me outgrow my tools."**

Every piece of KB-targeted copy should reflect this mindset. They don't need convincing that online education works — they need to see that Teachable can keep up with where they're going.

## How to write for KBs

### Lead with outcomes, not features
KBs aren't browsing — they're evaluating. They want to know *what changes for their business*, not just what buttons exist.

**Instead of:** "Teachable offers course bundles."
**Write:** "Package your expertise into bundles that increase average order value — without adding complexity to your workflow."

### Respect their sophistication
These are people running real businesses. They understand AOV, churn, LTV, and conversion funnels. You don't need to over-explain business concepts — but you should connect Teachable features to the business metrics they already track.

**Instead of:** "You can set different prices for your courses."
**Write:** "Test pricing strategies across your catalog — subscriptions, payment plans, one-time purchases — and find what drives the most revenue per student."

### Mirror their ambition
KBs are growth-oriented. They want to scale, expand into new segments, and build something lasting. Copy should feel like it's pulling them forward, not just describing the status quo.

**Good:** "Scale your education business worldwide — with tools that grow as you do."
**Avoid:** "Teachable is a great place to sell your course."

### Acknowledge their pain without dwelling on it
KBs are frustrated by platforms that are either too basic or too enterprise-heavy. A quick nod to this tension builds credibility, but don't spend three paragraphs on their problems — move quickly to the solution.

**Good:** "You shouldn't have to choose between 'simple but limited' and 'powerful but overwhelming.' Teachable gives you both."

## Feature prioritization for KBs

When writing for KBs, foreground the features that map to their goals. Not every feature matters equally — here's a ranked guide:

### Tier 1 — Always relevant (lead with these)
These connect directly to KB goals of revenue growth, brand authority, and scalability:
- Bundles, upsells, and order bumps (revenue growth)
- Professional student dashboard and branded experience (brand equity)
- Certificates and course compliance (authority + outcomes)
- Multi-product catalog with flexible pricing (scaling the business)
- Coupons, payment plans, subscriptions, BNPL (conversion optimization)

### Tier 2 — Strong supporting features
These reinforce operational simplicity and student engagement:
- Drag-and-drop course builder and AI tools (time savings)
- Automated tax handling and affiliate payouts (operational simplicity)
- iOS & Android mobile apps (student experience)
- Quizzes, drip content, multiple content types (engagement)
- Abandoned cart recovery (revenue recovery)

### Tier 3 — Situationally relevant
Use these when the copy targets a specific sub-persona or use case:
- Bulk licensing, SSO, multi-admin (for KBs moving into B2B / Continuing Ed sub-persona)
- Third-party integrations, API, webhooks (for KBs with established tech stacks)
- Learning pathways (upcoming — use when discussing product roadmap or forward-looking copy)

## Sub-persona targeting

Not all KBs are identical. When you know (or can infer) which sub-persona you're writing for, adjust emphasis:

### Individual Experts
Solo operators whose name IS the brand. Emphasize: time savings, professional branding, ease of managing everything alone, scaling beyond 1:1. They feel the "too many spreadsheets" pain acutely.

**Tone:** Peer-to-peer, empowering. "You've built the expertise — we handle the rest."

### Member Associations
Small teams building an education brand bigger than any one person. Emphasize: multi-product catalog, team collaboration features, operational efficiency at scale, analytics.

**Tone:** Solution-focused, slightly more operational. "Run a growing education business without growing your headcount."

### Continuing Education & Credentialing Providers
Organizations delivering mandatory training, exams, or certifications. Emphasize: certificates, compliance settings, bulk licensing, SSO, dedicated support. These prospects often come through sales-led motions.

**Tone:** Professional, trust-building, compliance-aware. "Deliver accredited training at scale — with the compliance tools your learners' careers depend on."

## Key messaging angles (with examples)

These are the three approved KB messaging pillars from the ICP doc. Use them as starting points, not rigid templates — adapt to channel and context.

### 1. Student engagement → Business results
The angle: happy, engaged students become repeat buyers and referral engines. Transformation drives trust, and trust drives revenue.

**Landing page example:** "Your students don't just finish courses — they come back for more. With quizzes, certificates, mobile access, and a polished dashboard, you create learning experiences that build loyalty and lifetime value."

**Ad example:** "Engaged students buy again. Build courses that drive real results — and real revenue."

### 2. Time savings and ease of use
The angle: KBs are busy. They want powerful tools that don't require a full ops team to manage. Teachable saves time so they can focus on what they do best — teaching and creating.

**Landing page example:** "Stop wrestling with your platform and start growing your business. From drag-and-drop course building to automated tax filing, Teachable handles the operational work so you don't have to."

**Ad example:** "More teaching, less admin. Teachable automates the busywork so you can focus on what matters."

### 3. Flexibility and integration
The angle: KBs have established workflows. They don't want to rip and replace — they want a platform that fits into their existing stack and grows with them.

**Landing page example:** "You've already built systems that work for you. Teachable integrates with your website, email provider, and analytics tools — so you can scale without starting over."

**Ad example:** "Your tools. Your workflow. Teachable fits right in — and helps you grow from there."

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Writing for beginners.** KBs are past the "should I create a course?" stage. Don't explain what online courses are or why education businesses are a good idea.
- **Leading with price.** KBs evaluate on capability and ROI, not just cost. Pricing can be a supporting point, but it shouldn't lead.
- **Ignoring sub-personas.** "Individual Expert" copy doesn't resonate with a "Continuing Ed" buyer and vice versa. When possible, know which sub-persona you're targeting.
- **Being too generic.** "Teachable helps you sell courses" is true but forgettable. Ground every claim in a specific KB goal or pain point.
- **Community-first framing.** KBs prioritize structured learning over community. If community is the main pitch, you're probably writing for Audience Builders, not KBs.
