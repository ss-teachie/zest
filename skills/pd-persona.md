# Program Distributor Persona Guide

This skill helps Teachable team members write copy and messaging that resonates with Program Distributor (PD) personas — our primary growth-bet acquisition target alongside Knowledge Businesses. It works as a companion to the `brand-and-style-guide` skill: use that skill for voice, tone, and formatting rules; use this skill to understand *who* you're writing for and *what* matters to them.

## When to use this skill

- Writing or reviewing copy aimed at PDs (landing pages, sales collateral, ads, emails, enterprise pages)
- Answering questions about PD motivations, pain points, or feature priorities
- Choosing which features to highlight and how to frame them for a B2B training buyer
- Tailoring generic Teachable messaging to speak specifically to organizations distributing training at scale
- Deciding which sub-persona (Corporation, Member Association, or Nonprofit) to target within a piece of content

## The PD mental model

Program Distributors think of education as **operations infrastructure**, not a product they sell. Training is how they onboard, certify, align, and standardize. They care about consistency, accountability, and scale — and they evaluate platforms the way an ops leader evaluates any business tool: does it reduce complexity, integrate with what we already use, and give us the reporting we need?

The emotional core: **"I need this to work reliably for thousands of people — and I need to prove it did."**

This is fundamentally different from a Knowledge Business. KBs ask "How do I grow my education business?" PDs ask "How do I get 5,000 employees trained by Q3 — and show the board it happened?" Every piece of PD-targeted copy should reflect this operational, accountability-driven mindset.

## How PD copy differs from KB copy

Understanding the contrast with KB messaging is critical, since the brand skill and other persona skills may be active simultaneously. Here are the key shifts:

| Dimension | Knowledge Business | Program Distributor |
|---|---|---|
| **Relationship to education** | Education IS the product | Education IS the operations |
| **Primary buyer motivation** | Revenue growth, brand equity | Risk reduction, compliance, efficiency |
| **Decision maker** | Founder, Creator, CEO | L&D Director, Training Manager, COO |
| **Evaluation criteria** | Commerce features, student experience, brand control | Scale, reporting, integrations, security |
| **Competitive set** | Other creator platforms (Kajabi, Thinkific) | Legacy LMS platforms (Cornerstone, SAP Litmos, Docebo) |
| **Tone** | Aspirational, empowering, peer-to-peer | Professional, trust-building, ROI-focused |
| **CTA style** | "Start for free" / "Explore plans" | "Request a demo" / "Contact sales" / "Book a call" |

## How to write for PDs

### Lead with operational outcomes
PDs don't care about "building a beautiful course." They care about getting 2,000 people trained consistently, tracking completion, and reporting on it. Frame everything through the lens of what it does for the organization.

**Instead of:** "Create engaging online courses."
**Write:** "Deliver consistent, structured training to thousands — with built-in tracking to prove it happened."

### Position against legacy LMS pain
PDs' primary alternative isn't another creator platform — it's an enterprise LMS. The key insight: those platforms are powerful but painfully complex, expensive, and slow to implement. Teachable's advantage is getting enterprise-grade results without enterprise-grade complexity.

**Instead of:** "Teachable is easy to use."
**Write:** "Get up and running in weeks, not months — without the dedicated IT team a traditional LMS demands."

### Speak the language of accountability
PDs report to leadership, boards, funders, or regulators. Completion tracking, certificates, and exportable data aren't nice-to-haves — they're how PDs prove their programs are working. Lean into the language of compliance, verification, and proof.

**Good:** "Know exactly who completed what — with exportable reports your compliance team can trust."
**Avoid:** "Track your students' progress."

### Address security and reliability upfront
Unlike KBs, PDs often have procurement and IT security teams involved in the buying decision. Mentioning SOC 2, SSO, GDPR, and uptime isn't optional — it's expected. These aren't bullet points to bury; for PDs, they're deal-qualifying criteria.

### Use ROI and cost framing
PDs justify purchases through business cases. Disengagement costs, time-to-onboard, and cost-per-learner are the metrics that unlock budget. When relevant, quantify the value — or at least frame Teachable's pricing model as an advantage over per-seat LMS pricing.

**Good:** "Enterprise plans start at 50K students — no per-seat surcharges eating into your training budget."

## Feature prioritization for PDs

When writing for PDs, foreground the features that map to their goals. The hierarchy is quite different from KB copy:

### Tier 1 — Always relevant (lead with these)
These connect directly to PD goals of scale, accountability, and operational simplicity:
- Bulk access and distribution (scale)
- Progress tracking and exportable reporting (accountability/compliance)
- Certificates and completion compliance (proof of training)
- SSO and role-based access (security and IT integration)
- Multi-admin controls (operational management)

### Tier 2 — Strong supporting features
These reinforce the "modern, reliable platform" positioning:
- iOS and Android apps (accessible anywhere)
- Quizzes, assessments, and knowledge checkpoints (validation)
- Gated/structured content progression (consistency)
- APIs and webhooks (integration with HR/IT stack)
- White-glove onboarding and dedicated CSM (premium support)

### Tier 3 — Situationally relevant
Use these when the copy targets a specific sub-persona or addresses a specific objection:
- SOC 2, PCI DSS, GDPR, CCPA compliance (for security-conscious procurement teams)
- Cost-per-learner advantage vs. legacy LMS (for budget justification)
- Upcoming: SCORM, admin roles 2.0, enterprise API (for roadmap-sensitive prospects — note SCORM is H2 2026)
- Learning pathways (upcoming — for guided, multi-course programs)

### Features to de-emphasize for PDs
These are KB-centric and can confuse PD messaging if foregrounded:
- Bundles, upsells, order bumps (commerce-focused, not relevant to internal training)
- Coupons, payment plans, BNPL (PDs aren't selling courses to consumers)
- Abandoned cart recovery (same — this is a storefront feature)
- Third-party sales page support (PDs don't have sales pages)

## Sub-persona targeting

Not all PDs are identical. When you know (or can infer) which sub-persona you're writing for, adjust emphasis:

### Corporations (L&D / Enablement)
Organizations training employees, partners, or channel teams. Emphasize: speed of implementation, integrations with existing HR/IT tools, employee engagement metrics, cost savings vs. legacy LMS.

**Tone:** ROI-driven, efficiency-focused. "Train your workforce at scale — without the 6-month implementation cycle."

**Key pain they feel:** Legacy LMS platforms are over-engineered for what they need. They want something that works out of the box.

### Member Associations (Continuing Education / Certification)
Organizations managing recurring certification or credentialing programs. Emphasize: certificates, compliance tracking, learner accountability, structured progression, renewals.

**Tone:** Trust-building, compliance-aware. "Deliver accredited programs with built-in tracking your board can rely on."

**Key pain they feel:** Piecing together completion data manually to satisfy regulators or accreditation bodies.

### Nonprofits (Program Delivery / Mission-Aligned Education)
Organizations delivering training to staff, volunteers, or communities in support of their mission. Emphasize: cost-effectiveness, ease of use for non-technical teams, reliable access across geographies, reporting for funders.

**Tone:** Mission-supportive, practical. "Extend your impact with training that reaches every community you serve."

**Key pain they feel:** Limited budgets and technical resources, but high accountability to funders and stakeholders.

## Messaging angles (with examples)

These are the core PD value propositions. Unlike KB messaging (which has three formally approved pillars), PD key messaging is still TK in the ICP doc — so these angles are derived from the ICP profile and the Scalable Training page. Treat them as working frameworks.

### 1. Enterprise capability, not enterprise complexity
The angle: PDs need what an LMS offers (structure, compliance, reporting) but not the baggage (months of implementation, dedicated IT staff, per-seat pricing that scales painfully).

**Landing page example:** "Enterprise learning without the enterprise price tag. Deliver structured training, development, and certification programs at scale — without the unnecessary complexity or cost of traditional LMS platforms."

**Ad example:** "Your team needed training yesterday. Legacy LMS says 'see you in six months.' Teachable says 'let's go.'"

### 2. Accountability and proof
The angle: PDs are accountable to leadership, funders, or regulators. They need to prove training happened, was completed, and stuck. Reporting and certificates aren't nice-to-haves — they're the whole point.

**Landing page example:** "Know exactly who completed what — with certificates, progress reports, and exportable data your compliance team can trust."

**Ad example:** "Your board asks 'Is the team trained?' Don't scramble for spreadsheets. Show them the dashboard."

### 3. Scale without chaos
The angle: PDs are managing hundreds or thousands of learners, often rotating in and out. Bulk enrollment, role-based access, and centralized controls prevent the administrative chaos that comes with doing this manually.

**Landing page example:** "Enroll thousands in one click. Segment by team, role, or region. Manage it all from a single dashboard — no spreadsheets required."

**Ad example:** "5,000 learners. 12 regions. One platform. Scale your training without scaling your admin team."

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Using KB framing.** PDs don't "monetize expertise" or "build an audience." They train, certify, and standardize. If your copy sounds like it's talking to a course creator, you're targeting the wrong persona.
- **Leading with student engagement.** For KBs, engaged students = repeat buyers. For PDs, engagement matters but it's in service of completion and compliance, not revenue. Frame engagement through the accountability lens.
- **Ignoring the buying committee.** PD purchases involve multiple stakeholders — training managers, IT/security, procurement, leadership. Copy aimed at a single decision-maker misses the reality of how these deals close.
- **Forgetting the competitive frame.** PDs aren't comparing Teachable to Kajabi or Thinkific. They're comparing to Cornerstone, SAP Litmos, Docebo, or doing it all in-house with spreadsheets. Position against *those* alternatives.
- **Over-promising on compliance.** Teachable does NOT have HIPAA, FERPA, or SCORM (until H2 2026). Don't imply otherwise. Lead with what we do have (SOC 2, PCI DSS, GDPR, CCPA) and be honest about gaps if asked.
- **Treating all PDs the same.** A corporate L&D team, a nursing board, and a nonprofit have very different motivations. When possible, know which sub-persona you're targeting.

## Handling known gaps honestly

PDs are sophisticated buyers who do due diligence. When writing copy, be aware of these current limitations so you don't overstate capabilities:

- **SCORM support** is not available until H2 2026 at the earliest. Don't promise it. If asked, acknowledge the roadmap timeline.
- **Branched learning paths and prerequisites** are not currently supported. Some PDs will need these.
- **Native HRIS/SIS integrations** (BambooHR, Workday, etc.) are not available. We offer API/webhook-based custom integration instead.
- **HIPAA and FERPA compliance** are not supported. This is a hard deal breaker for some healthcare and education-sector PDs.

When these come up in copy or prospect-facing materials, don't avoid them — but don't volunteer them either. Focus on what Teachable does well, and let sales handle objection management for specific deals.
